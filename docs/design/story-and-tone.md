# Story and Tone

[Back to the implementation plan](../implementation-plan.md)

## Premise

A spaceball card convention, somewhere in the Kuiper Belt, 199X. Space bounty
hunter **Sleve Bonzalez** is about to have his most prized possession graded:
a **1994 holo-foil Bobson Dugnutt rookie card**. Then the lights go out. When
they come back on, the card is gone, and so is **HotCoffee**, the android
barista who had been "just browsing" for six straight hours.

Sleve chases HotCoffee's ship to **VetroMania** (*vetro* is Italian for
glass). It's a gothic planet of stained-glass cathedrals, steel buttresses,
and a truly irresponsible number of monsters. Sleve's ship, *The Dugnutt*,
takes a hit on approach and crash-lands. His gear is repossessed on the spot.
He has a bat, a catcher's-mitt arm cannon, a nagging drone, and a mullet.

### The MacGuffin makes sense, technically

The planet's ancient builders, the Vetrans, kept VetroMania right side up with
a **Gravity Organ**: a planet-sized pipe organ at the core, playing one
eternal symphony. The Vetrans stored the organ's score on the most durable
medium they knew: collectible trading cards. That set was later reprinted,
with spelling errors, as the *Vetro Fighting Spaceballers*.

HotCoffee is a completionist. He owns every card in the set except one. With
Sleve's Bobson Dugnutt rookie, he can play the **Symphony of the Spaceball**,
which turns the planet upside down. He has not thought much about what
happens after that.

## Story beats

### Prologue: SpaceCon '94 (10 minutes)

- Sleve starts at **full power** (a Lv 50 preview kit with every ability) to
  sell the fantasy, just as Symphony of the Night opens with Alucard at his
  peak.
- He chases HotCoffee through the asteroid belt and fights **Latte-Tron**, a
  drone boss, as the Seal tutorial.
- *The Dugnutt* crashes. Repo-Man repossesses everything. "IT'S DANGEROUS TO
  GO ALONE. TAKE THIS BAT," says a vending machine.

### Act 1: The Hull (hours 0–3)

- Crash Site Z-0 teaches movement and combat, then Hull Cathedral opens as the
  hub. The **Espresso Spire** looms over it and is visible from almost
  everywhere, like Dracula's keep.
- Deckard Cane explains the Gravity Organ and the card set.
- The Spire is sealed. Its door needs four Autograph Seals.

### Act 2: The Symphony (hours 3–9)

- Sleve collects abilities and seals across Outer Hull, Bio-Lab Lambda,
  Server Chapel, Cryo Caverns, Long Data Archive, and Laser Colosseum.
- HotCoffee taunts him from screens and posters. The card shows up in
  cutscenes behind glass, in a toploader, in a humidor, and once in a
  microwave.
- NAG-9000 keeps mentioning "some kind of glasses" that could help. Most
  players will ignore it.

### Act 3: The Spire (hours 9–12)

- Chrono Clocktower and the Arcade Necropolis. Kill Screen drops the **Holy
  Shades**, which are optional.
- HotCoffee fights in three phases: Drip, Pour-Over, and French Press.
- **Bad ending (no Holy Shades):** Sleve wins the card back and it's a
  misprint: "BOBSON DUGNUT." Credits roll with "BUT THE FUTURE REFUSED TO
  CHANGE," then a hint: *"TRY LOOKING AT THINGS DIFFERENTLY."*
- **True path (Holy Shades on):** the Shades reveal puppet strings running
  from HotCoffee up into the sky. Cut them and COLD BREW steps out of the
  reflection, grabs the real card, and plays the Symphony. **The planet
  flips.**

### Act 4: Ainamortev (hours 12–26)

- The world is upside down, hue-shifted, and wrong. Every area has a remixed
  twin with new bosses and mirror enemies. The map percentage keeps counting
  past 100%: each planet contributes up to 100%.
- The Gravity Organ console in the Reverse Cathedral can flip the planet back
  and forth on demand, so nothing on either planet is ever missable.
- HotCoffee, freed and humiliated, reappears as a reluctant ally. He runs a
  pop-up cafe at the Launch Site and sells buffs.
- COLD BREW waits in the **Decaf Dungeon**, the inverted Espresso Spire. His
  fight needs Seal 5.

### True ending

- Sleve gets the card back, signed: *"TO SLEVE: KEEP YOUR EYE ON THE BALL. -
  B.D."* The organ plays the Symphony in reverse, and the planet flips back.
- Post-credits: HotCoffee's new cafe, "NOW SERVING DECAF." A teaser card
  reads: **VETROMANIA 2: BOBSON'S REVENGE. COMING 199X.**
- Completion bonus (Metroid style): clear in under 3 hours and Sleve takes
  off his helmet at the end. He is wearing sunglasses under his sunglasses.

## Tone bible

### The target

VetroMania is a sincere Metroidvania wearing a very stupid hat. The *DudeBro 2*
reference sets the comic voice: macho-game bravado played completely straight
until it collapses under its own weight. Absurdly long item names, a hero who
takes spaceball more seriously than death, and a villain who monologues
about foam.

The *game* never plays for laughs. Controls, hitboxes, boss telegraphs, and
map clarity are held to Dawn of Sorrow's standard. The world around them is
where the jokes go.

### Guardrails

1. **Punch up or sideways.** Targets are games, genre tropes, 90s tech,
   corporate culture, bro culture, and Sleve's ego. Never real tragedies,
   identities, protected classes, or real people.
2. **The grandma test.** A joke must work for someone who has never heard of
   the reference. The reference is a bonus layer, not the punchline.
3. **Crude, not cruel.** Profanity, bodily humor, and bro bravado are fine in
   Salty mode. Slurs are never fine, in any mode.
4. **Never at the player's expense.** No fake crashes or fake save deletions,
   and no joke that costs progress. A fake-out is allowed at most once, and
   only if it's instantly obvious.
5. **Parody, not copy.** Transform every reference: new art, new words, and no
   copied melodies, logos, or lyrics. The [reference ledger](reference-ledger.md)
   tracks each one with a risk tag.
6. **Callbacks beat new jokes.** Set up running gags (NAG-9000's tutorials,
   the card-in-a-microwave cutscenes, the Roster's names) and pay them off.

### Density

| Channel | Budget |
| --- | --- |
| Background props (posters, graffiti, screens) | At most 1 per room; about 1 in 3 rooms have one |
| Enemy and item names | Every one, since this is the cheapest channel |
| Item flavor text | Every rare, legendary, and set item |
| NPC dialogue | At most 1 gag per exchange; exposition stays readable |
| NAG-9000 barks | Only when triggered by something; never on a timer |
| Fourth-wall breaks | At most 1 per area |
| Death messages and loading tips | Rotating pool of 60 or more, never repeated back to back |

### Language modes

On first launch NAG-9000 asks: *"HOW DO YOU TAKE YOUR BOUNTY HUNTER: SALTY OR
DECAF?"*

- **Salty:** full DudeBro vocabulary, within the ESRB T limit.
- **Decaf:** profanity is replaced with grawlix (`@#$%!`) in the style of
  Q\*bert, and spoken lines get a slide-whistle bleep.

Either mode can be changed at any time in the options.

### Sample lines

These calibrate the voice. Each is shown in Decaf mode.

> **HOTCOFFEE:** WHAT IS A CARD? A MISERABLE LITTLE PILE OF CARDBOARD! BUT
> ENOUGH TALK. HAVE AT YOU, AND ALSO HAVE A LOYALTY PUNCH CARD.

> **SLEVE:** DIE, MONSTER! YOU DON'T BELONG IN THIS WORLD! ...WAIT. THIS IS
> YOUR PLANET, ISN'T IT? THAT'S ON ME.

> **NAG-9000:** HEY! LISTEN! THAT WALL SOUNDS HOLLOW. ALSO, YOU HAVE 212 UNREAD
> TUTORIALS.

> **DECKARD CANE:** STAY AWHILE AND LISTEN. NO, REALLY. MY HIP IS DONE. I
> CANNOT LEAVE.

> **REPO-MAN:** YOUR JETPACK, YOUR SUIT, YOUR BAT. PAYMENTS WERE 900 YEARS
> OVERDUE. I'LL LEAVE YOU THE MULLET. I'M NOT A MONSTER.

> **SLEVE**, *on reaching Lv 50:* I NEVER ASKED FOR THIS. ...I MEAN, I DID
> GRIND FOR THIS. FOR ELEVEN HOURS.

> **Item flavor**, *Overclocked Mag-Boots of the Leeroy:* "AT LEAST I HAVE
> CHICKEN."

> **Death message:** YOU HAVE DIED OF SPACE DYSENTERY.

### Review process

- The writer drafts. The director checks each joke against the guardrails.
  Anything referencing real products or people goes into the ledger with a
  risk tag before it ships in a build.
- Every playtest survey asks: *"Did any joke make you cringe for the wrong
  reason?"* Any line flagged by 3 or more testers gets rewritten.
- Localization gets a joke glossary: for each pun, the intent, the reference,
  and permission to replace it with a local equivalent.
